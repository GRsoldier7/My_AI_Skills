## Seven written trials on November 21

In a manual, text-only trial, one reader used each restored October package and its references on one fictional prompt. A second reader scored the resulting written answers before the seven edits below. Accuracy and biblical context carry 30 points, audience and task fit 25, practical usefulness 20, economy and clarity 15, and care and privacy 10. The totals are judgments about these answers. A one-point difference provides little basis for preferring one skill for another skill's job.

For example, the Philippians study ended by asking what dependence on Christ would look like the morning after either interview outcome. In the Psalm 13 draft, the public invitation asked readers which part of the prayer gave them words for today. The written samples are drafts for review. The study leader still decides whether the question fits the people in the room.

These samples used no live publishing, private journals, church participants, or target-host installations. They provide no elapsed-time measurement, repeated-run reliability estimate, or score for the seven new edits. To judge time saved, time one ordinary preparation on the usual tool, repeat with the corresponding skill, and record minutes and substantive corrections privately. Use different passages on later trials.

## Two teaching trials after the edits

A new reader used the revised skills on different passages. The adult guide for Ephesians 4:25–32 connected the leader's three discussion notes to particular verses about speech, anger and forgiveness. The children's lesson for Luke 15:1–7 used the listeners' grumbling in verses 1–2 and Jesus' explanation in verse 7 to explain the surprise. Each plan included a learning check. Both schedules had seven consecutive segments totaling the requested 12 minutes. These are written lesson plans. Classroom pacing and learning have yet to be measured.
