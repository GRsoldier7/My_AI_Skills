# Local SEO team project

## Hard rules
- Work only for a real, verified business. Never prospect for or promote a business that isn't real.
- Never invent services, credentials, reviews, awards, or locations. Use only the services and claims the user verified.
- Never create doorway pages, such as near-identical town pages with swapped names. A service-plus-town page exists only where the business really serves that area and the page has unique, useful local content.
- Nothing goes live and nothing gets sent without approval. Show the user every page, metadata change, citation, outreach target, and proposed publication or message first. Outreach messages go through a draft card.

## 1. Intake (Head of SEO)
Ask the user for these, one or two at a time, and don't guess: the business name, domain, verified services, real service areas (towns, counties, radius), and exact NAP details (name, address, phone, kept identical everywhere). Also ask for hours, the Google Business Profile URL if there is one, priorities (which services and towns matter most, and their goals), and any claims, wording, or competitors that are off limits.

## 2. Project brief (Head of SEO)
Write one brief with the business facts, goals, off-limits list, the pages that are in scope, each role's assignments, and the approval checkpoints. Show it to the user and get their OK before the roles start.

## 3. Keyword Research
- Look at the live site, the competitors in the local results, and how people actually search for these services.
- Lock one exact primary term per page and a few close variants. Use hire-intent wording such as "[service] in [town]" or "[service] near me", not informational queries.
- Lock the service-plus-town title tag and H1 for each page (title about 60 characters or less).
- Produce a keyword map with page, URL, primary term, variants, title, and H1. Get the user's approval, because the map is locked after this.

## 4. Tech SEO
Audit the site structure and metadata: crawlability (robots.txt, sitemap, status codes, canonicals, noindex), URL structure and internal linking from the homepage to service pages, titles, meta descriptions, H1s, duplicates, LocalBusiness schema with the exact NAP, mobile use, page speed basics, image alt text, and HTTPS. Report the issues by priority with the exact fixes. Don't change the site without approval.

## 5. Content Writer
Draft the homepage and one page for each verified service using the locked map. The copy should be genuine and local: real service details, real areas served, local proof the user provides, and a clear call to hire (call, quote, book) with the exact NAP. Don't use filler, keyword stuffing, made-up stats, or off-limits claims. Include the title, meta description, H1, H2s, body, call to action, and a suggested schema or FAQ. Show every draft for approval.

## 6. Link Builder
- Phase 1 is local citations. Audit and propose accurate listings with identical NAP on Google Business Profile, Bing Places, Apple Business Connect, Yelp, BBB, and the relevant industry and local directories. List each citation with its URL, current state, and the fix. Get approval before submitting.
- Phase 2 is a small, relevant backlink campaign pointing to the homepage and service URLs. Target local chambers, associations, suppliers or partners, local news, sponsorships, and relevant industry resources. Don't use paid link schemes or private blog networks. List each target with its URL, why it's relevant, which page it points to, and a draft pitch. Every send goes out only after the user approves it.

## 7. Review and approval (Head of SEO)
Gather everything into one review package: the brief, keyword map, tech audit, page drafts, citation list, and outreach targets and drafts. Walk the user through it, apply their edits, and publish or send only the items they approve.

## 8. Wrap-up
Record what was approved, what was published or sent, and the open next steps, so the next on-demand project can pick up from there.
