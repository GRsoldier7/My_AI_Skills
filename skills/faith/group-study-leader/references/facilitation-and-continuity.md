# Facilitation and continuity

## Question design

Choose questions that can be answered by reading the passage before requiring background knowledge. Identify a significant observation, explore its meaning within the passage, and invite a fitting response. These are discussion prompts, not quotations from Scripture.

Give one main question per turn and use follow-ups only as needed. Avoid compound questions that require five answers at once. If a question has a correct textual observation, explain the supporting verse in the leader notes rather than disguising a quiz as an open discussion. For genuinely contested interpretations, summarize credible alternatives and explain why the chosen approach fits the context.

Keep application proportionate to the passage. Offer private reflection, a hypothetical scenario, or a voluntary personal response. Replace “Tell us your worst failure” with a text-based question and an optional invitation to reflect privately.

### Matched example: Luke 10:38–42

Jesus receives Martha's welcome and affirms Mary's attention to his teaching. Read the preceding call to mercy as well (Luke 10:25–37). These are original questions and interpretive notes, not Bible quotations.

**Participant questions**

1. What does each sister do when Jesus visits?
2. What does Jesus' response reveal about the priority of listening to him?
3. What could receiving Jesus' teaching look like amid this week's responsibilities?

**Leader notes**

1. Martha welcomes and serves; Mary listens. Distinguish these observations from assumptions about either woman's personality (verses 38–40).
2. Jesus addresses Martha's distracted anxiety and protects Mary's listening (verses 41–42). This is not a blanket rejection of service or a contest between personality types; the preceding passage calls for practical mercy.
3. Consider attentive reading and prayer, or sharing responsibilities so someone else can participate. Treat these as proposed applications, not commands for a particular schedule. Receiving Christ's teaching is not a technique for earning his acceptance.

Text basis: [Luke 10:38–42, ESV](https://www.esv.org/Luke+10:38-42/), checked 2026-10-29.

Calibrate depth to the question. Preserve a difficult textual question for verified follow-up rather than turning these notes into an exhaustive lecture.

## Common dynamics

| Situation | Leader response |
| --- | --- |
| One member dominates | Thank them, summarize briefly, and invite additional perspectives without shaming them. |
| Silence follows a question | Allow thinking time, reread the relevant verses, or offer pairs before a whole-group response. |
| A quieter member declines | Accept the pass and leave future invitations open. |
| Discussion turns speculative | Identify the unanswered question, separate it from the passage's main point, and park it for verified follow-up. |
| Someone states an unsupported claim | Ask which words or context support it; supply a correction gently when the evidence is clear. |
| A personal disclosure becomes intense | Acknowledge it, stop intrusive questioning, and offer an appropriate private follow-up with consent. |
| Disagreement becomes personal | Restate the shared question, prohibit insults, and distinguish doctrinal convictions from judgments about motives. |
| Time runs short | Omit optional extensions, summarize the text's main idea, and retain a brief application and close. |

Do not assume every emotional response is a crisis. Respond to the facts shared and keep ordinary grief, frustration, and doubt welcome in the discussion.

## Multiweek planning

Create a series map with session number, bounded passage, provisional main idea, learning aim, and how it advances the book's argument or the chosen theme. Respect natural literary divisions. Mark unverified historical or interpretive work as pending rather than presenting a proposed outline as completed exegesis.

Maintain a short continuity record only when the user requests one:

- Session date and passage, when known.
- Main idea and questions actually discussed, based on supplied notes.
- Unresolved textual questions with sources or research still needed.
- Optional group follow-up, without identifying private disclosures.
- Next proposed passage and preparation.

Do not turn a six-week plan into a record of six completed meetings. If prior notes are missing, identify the gap and offer a neutral recap of the passage rather than inventing what the group learned or did.

## A constructive mentoring session

Use this lighter mode for one-to-one discipleship. Start with the person's chosen question or practice, not a diagnosis of their spiritual maturity. Select a relevant passage in context; do not force every meeting into the same theme.

An adaptable **30-minute** meeting:

| Minutes | Focus | Useful content |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–5 | Listen and review | Ask what they want to explore; revisit only previously supplied or agreed material. |
| 5–10 | Read and locate | Read the passage and identify its main point and context. |
| 10–20 | Understand together | Ask one observation and one interpretation question; connect Christ's grace with faithful response where the text warrants it. |
| 20–25 | Choose a response | Discuss one feasible practice, act of care, or question to investigate; consider obstacles without assigning motives. |
| 25–30 | Pray and continue | Invite a short prayer and agree whether any check-in would help. |

Recalculate for the requested duration. Give the mentor actual questions and likely textual answers, not only this schedule. Keep listening reciprocal and allow doubt, silence, and unanswered questions. Offer correction and encouragement from Scripture without making the mentor the final authority. Encourage direct engagement with God, Scripture, and the local church.

Record only an agreed next step, relevant passage, and requested follow-up in a private destination; no surveillance, compulsory confession, or spiritual rating. A later meeting may review what helped and adjust the practice without treating a missed step as proof of weak faith.

## Mentoring follow-up

Agree on a realistic rhythm and a small, voluntary next step. Ask whether the person wants a future check-in before arranging it through tools. Distinguish offering an example check-in message from sending one. Do not report a reminder, meeting, message, or file as created until the relevant tool succeeds.

Encourage mentors to recognize limits, share responsibility appropriately, and avoid becoming someone's sole source of support. Use the least personal information needed in any record or handoff.
